## Detailed Feedback & Growth Areas

### 1. Baselines & Benchmark Coverage (Empirical Evaluation)

- **Specific Text Example:** _Section V-C & Table I ("We compare all three hierarchical algorithms... against PDM, FM and CTA heuristics as well as non-hierarchical baselines IAC, SNAC, and SEAC")_
- **Issue:** While comparing against industry heuristics (PDM, FM, CTA) and actor-critic MARL baselines (IAC, SNAC, SEAC) demonstrates the utility of hierarchy, it overlooks value-factorization techniques dominant in multi-robot task allocation and path management. Specifically, value-based cooperative frameworks like **QMIX** (with action masking) are widely recognized benchmarks for centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) in warehouse operations.
- **Actionable Next Step:** How does your hierarchical actor-critic setup compare to value-factorization algorithms such as QMIX when both are equipped with identical action-masking techniques? Adding a reference or baseline comparison against QMIX-based warehouse control schemes will strengthen the paper's theoretical positioning.

### 2. Formalization of Hierarchy & Spatial Action Spaces

- **Specific Text Example:** _Section IV-B ("The manager's action space consists of a choice from the set of zones $Y$ given by $\mathcal{A}^m = Y^{\vert{}\mathcal{I}\vert{}}$...")_
- **Issue:** The temporal dynamics between the Manager layer and the Worker layer are slightly ambiguous. The paper states that workers execute actions until reaching target locations using $A^*$, but Equation (1) sums rewards over variable worker horizons $k_i$. The exact semi-MDP (SMDP) or macro-action transition dynamics governing manager re-decisions need explicit formalization.
- **Actionable Next Step:** Can you explicitly define the temporal decision epoch for the Manager? Formulating the Manager level as a formal Options Framework or Semi-POSG (S-POSG) will clarify how asynchronous worker completions ($k_i$) trigger Manager updates.

### 3. Inventory & Dynamic Allocation Context Integration

- **Specific Text Example:** _Section III-A ("In GTP picking, multiple AGVs... carry separate storage mediums... required in an order $z$")_
- **Issue:** The paper focuses heavily on travel-time minimization and pick rate, treating order allocation as static inputs ($z \sim Z$). In real-world fulfillment, stock allocation, replenishment delays, and shared holding capacities (as seen in multi-node supply chain literature) dictate real-time congestion and destination priorities.
- **Actionable Next Step:** Would it be beneficial to frame the upper-layer Manager not only as a spatial partitioner, but as an adaptive task-and-inventory distributor? Elaborating on how stock availability impacts action-masking constraints will bridge the gap between pure MAPF/MARL and supply-chain logistics.

### 2. Section IV-B: Hierarchical Decomposition Formalism
- **Original:**
  "The manager reward $r_t^m$ is the sum of the rewards from all the assigned goals to non-busy workers $i \in \mathcal{I}$ during a timestep."
- **Suggested Edit:**
  <comment-tag author="Senior Editor" note="Refined mathematical definition of the macro-action reward transition for the Manager agent.">
  "The Manager operates on an asynchronous Semi-POSG formulation. The Manager reward $r_t^m$ at macro-step $t$ aggregates the cumulative temporal rewards accumulated by non-busy worker agents $i \in \mathcal{I}_{active}$ over their respective task execution intervals $k_i$:

  $$r_t^m = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{active}} \sum_{\tau=0}^{k_i - 1} \gamma^\tau r_{t+\tau}^i$$

  where $k_i$ denotes the temporal duration (in low-level execution steps) required for worker $i$ to reach target location $l^i \in y^i$ via the $A^*$ path-planner."
  </comment-tag>

### 3. Section V-C: Baseline & Related Architecture Discussion
- **Original:**
  "The sub-par performance of SNAC is due to all worker agents using identical policies, which can lead to delays incurred due to frequent collisions or deadlocks..."
- **Suggested Edit:**
  <comment-tag author="Senior Editor" note="Added discussion contextualizing value-factorization (QMIX) and action-masking interactions in dense fulfillment spaces.">
  "The sub-par performance of SNAC stems from uniform policy parameters across heterogeneous agent roles, leading to spatial deadlocks in high-density aisles. While centralized value-factorization algorithms (e.g., QMIX) address multi-agent credit assignment through mixing networks, they can suffer from scaling bottlenecks when action spaces $\vert{}\mathcal{A}\vert{}$ span thousands of discrete warehouse coordinates. Our hierarchical decomposition bypasses this by restricting value/policy updates to high-level spatial partitions $Y$, delegating local collision-free execution to $A^*$ low-level controllers."
